## CHAPTER 1: THE VEHICLE & DAILY MAINTENANCE ROUTINE

A concrete mixer is a mobile heavy manufacturing plant. If you neglect maintenance, the concrete will find your weak spot and destroy components that cost thousands of pounds to replace.

### 1.1 The Daily Greasing Routine (Never Skip This)
Concrete dust, wash-water, and heavy rotational loads strip grease faster than on any other HGV. Greasing must be done **daily**:

1. **Drum Roller Bearings (Trunnion Rollers):** 
   - 2 to 3 pumps of heavy-duty EP2 lithium grease on each rear roller bearing daily. 
   - Inspect the roller faces for flat spots, grooving, or bearing play. If a roller seizes, friction will wear a flat spot into your drum tyre ring in under 10 miles.
2. **Main Drum Pivot Bearing (Front Pedestal):**
   - Grease according to manufacturer spec (weekly or bi-weekly depending on grease point location).
3. **Chute Swivel & Pivot Pins:**
   - Pump grease into the swivel pivot daily. Concrete slurry dries inside the swivel, making the chute seize and causing severe driver back strain.
4. **Hydraulic Chute Jack & Mechanical Linkages:**
   - Light grease or oil on screw threads and locking latches.
5. **PTO & Drive Shaft Universal Joints (U-Joints):**
   - Check weekly. A failed PTO U-joint cuts hydraulic drive instantly, leaving you stranded with a full drum.

### 1.2 The Water Tank System
- **Daily Pre-Trip:** Check water tank level before leaving the yard. A mixer driver without water is a driver waiting to ruin a truck.
- **Pressurisation Check:** Ensure the air pressure regulator is holding correct operating pressure (typically 3.5 to 4.5 bar). Check the safety relief valve manually by pulling the ring.
- **Hose Inspection:** Check high-pressure wash hose for splits, nozzle leaks, and trigger lock integrity.

## CHAPTER 2: DRUM SPEED CONTROLS & ROTATIONAL DYNAMICS

The rotation speed and direction of your drum dictates concrete quality, engine fuel burn, drum fin wear, and rollover stability.

> **💡 The UK Driver's Direction Rule (Ian Henry):**
> - **From the front of the wagon (looking rearwards):** The drum turns **Clockwise** for mixing and agitating.
> - **From the driver's seat (drum behind you):** The drum turns **towards the Passenger Side (Nearside / Left-hand side)** for mixing and agitating, and towards the **Driver's Side (Offside / Right-hand side)** for discharging.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| OPERATING PHASE       | DRUM SPEED (RPM) | ROTATION DIRECTION (CAB / FRONT VIEW)           | PURPOSE          |
+-----------------------+------------------+-------------------------------------------------+------------------+
| Batch Loading         | 12 – 16 RPM      | Towards Passenger Side (Clockwise from front)   | Intake & mixing  |
| Yard Slump Adjustment | 12 – 14 RPM      | Towards Passenger Side (Clockwise from front)   | Blend admixtures |
| Road Transit (Wet)    | 2 – 4 RPM        | Towards Passenger Side (Clockwise from front)   | Agitation only   |
| 0-Slump / Semi-Dry    | 0 RPM (STOPPED)  | DRUM OFF (STATIONARY)                           | Prevents balling |
| Discharging (Pouring) | 8 – 14 RPM       | Towards Driver's Side (Anti-Clock from front)   | Smooth delivery  |
| Washout / Cleaning    | 12 – 14 RPM      | Alternating Both Directions                     | Scour blades     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2.1 Batch Loading Speed (12 – 16 RPM)
When standing under the batch cone, run the drum at **high charging speed (12–16 RPM)**. This pulls the dry aggregates, cement, and batch water down to the belly of the drum, creating a homogenous mix. 
- *Pro Tip:* Once loaded, hold at mixing speed for **at least 1 to 2 minutes (approx. 70–100 revolutions)** before scaling out to ensure complete batch consistency.

### 2.2 Transit / Agitation Speed for Wet Concrete (2 – 4 RPM) — The Golden Rule
**NEVER DRIVE ON THE ROAD AT FULL MIXING SPEED.**
- In transit with standard wet concrete, the drum must rotate at **Agitation Speed only (2 to 4 RPM)**.
- **Why?**
  1. High drum speed generates friction heat, rapidly drying out the concrete and accelerating slump loss.
  2. High drum speed causes severe centrifugal inertia, raising the vehicle's dynamic centre of gravity and dramatically increasing rollover risk on roundabouts.
  3. Running at 14 RPM on the road burns an extra 15–20% in diesel and causes premature blade wear.

### 2.3 The Zero-Slump / Semi-Dry Rule: TRAVEL WITH THE DRUM STOPPED (Ian Henry)
When carrying **0-slump concrete, dry-lean, kerb mix, or semi-dry mixes (ST1, Gen 0)**:
- **DISENGAGE THE PTO AND TRAVEL WITH THE DRUM COMPLETELY STOPPED (0 RPM).**
- **Why? The "Balling" Trap:**
  1. If you turn the drum on semi-dry mix during transit, the material behaves like wet snow rolling down a hill. It balls up into dense, rock-hard concrete "cannonballs" (often the size of footballs) that pack tightly behind the blades.
  2. These hardened concrete balls will **NOT** discharge down the chutes. You will arrive on site with a load you cannot pour, requiring hours of jackhammering or costly manual de-dagging.
  3. Continuous turning of dry aggregate generates intense friction heat with no free water to cool it, causing the cement paste to flash-set inside the drum.
- **The Correct Jobsite Procedure:**
  1. Batch and mix thoroughly at the plant at 14–16 RPM until uniform.
  2. Disengage PTO and stop the drum completely (0 RPM) before scaling out.
  3. Travel to the jobsite with the drum stationary.
  4. On arrival at the pour site, start the drum in **Mixing Speed (10–12 RPM) for 30 to 60 seconds** to loosen and remix the bed of material before reversing to discharge.

### 2.4 Discharge Speed (8 – 14 RPM in Reverse)
- Always start reversing the drum slowly (4–6 RPM) until concrete reaches the discharge cone.
- Increase speed to match groundworker or pump hopper demand.
- Stop rotation immediately if the chute or wheelbarrow overflows. Never leave the drum spinning in reverse unattended.

### 2.5 Emergency Protocol: Engine Stalls with Live Concrete
If your truck engine or PTO cuts out with a full 8m³ load:
1. **Act Immediately:** You have between **45 to 90 minutes** before concrete starts its initial set, depending on temperature and mix design.
2. **Alert Dispatch & Plant:** Call dispatch (`07494 867646`) and your batch plant immediately.
3. **Emergency Auxiliary Drive:** If an auxiliary hydraulic rescue pack or buddy truck hose is available, connect hydraulic jump lines to rotate the drum.
4. **Emergency Chemical Retarder (Sugar Pack):** Many fleets carry emergency sugar packs (white refined granulated sugar acts as a total concrete retarder). Dumping 25–50 kg of sugar with water into a full drum neutralises hydration and buys you 3 to 6 hours to get the load to a discharge point.

## CHAPTER 3: SLUMP GAUGES & VISUAL SLUMP TESTING

Understanding the wetness (consistency) of your concrete is the hallmark of a professional mixer driver.

### 3.1 Understanding the Hydraulic Slump Pressure Gauge
Every modern mixer has a hydraulic pressure gauge on the rear pedestal and inside the cab.
- **How It Works:** The gauge measures the hydraulic pressure (in bar or PSI) required to turn the drum.
  - Stiff, dry concrete creates heavy resistance $\rightarrow$ **High pressure reading**.
  - Wet, fluid concrete creates low resistance $\rightarrow$ **Low pressure reading**.
- **Crucial Rule:** The slump gauge reading is **relative to volume**. 8m³ of S3 slump will show much higher pressure than 2m³ of the exact same mix! Learn your gauge baseline for full, half, and quarter loads at 10 RPM.

### 3.2 Visual Slump Assessment Inside the Drum
Before you leave the weighbridge, climb the inspection ladder, look into the drum at mixing speed (10–12 RPM), and observe the concrete:

```
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| VISUAL CUE IN DRUM     | ESTIMATED SLUMP | MIX CHARACTERISTICS                           |
+------------------------+-----------------+-----------------------------------------------+
| Balling / Clumping     | Dry / S1        | Concrete breaks off blades in chunks; no flow |
| Rolling Curl / Folding | S2 (50–90mm)    | Folds smoothly like heavy dough; dull surface |
| Flowing Wave / Sheen   | S3 (100–150mm)  | Glides off blades; glossy wet sheen (Standard)|
| Fluid Liquid Ripple    | S4 (160–210mm)  | Level surface; ripples easily; pump mix       |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 3.3 Visual Slump on the Chute & Floor
- **S1 Consistency (10 – 40mm):** Stiff. Doesn't slump when dropped. Used for kerb-backing, paving foundations, and trench fill where early strength is needed.
- **S2 Consistency (50 – 90mm):** Semi-dry. Slumps slightly into a mound. Used for reinforced foundations and simple slabs.
- **S3 Consistency (100 – 150mm):** Normal workability. Slides easily down standard chutes without water addition. Perfect for house footings, domestic floors, and general building.
- **S4 Consistency (160 – 210mm):** Very fluid. Spreads across the trench with minimal raking. Typical for concrete pump pours.

### 3.4 The Official Slump Cone Test (BS EN 12350-2)
When testing on site:
1. Dampen the slump cone and base plate.
2. Fill the cone in **3 equal layers**.
3. Rod each layer **25 times** using a standard 16mm round-ended steel rod.
4. Strike off the top level with a rodding bar.
5. Lift the cone vertically in **2 to 5 seconds** without twisting.
6. Measure the true drop from the top of the cone to the displaced centre.
- *Types of Slump:* True Slump (Valid), Shear Slump (Mix sheared sideways — invalid, must re-test), Collapse Slump (Mix completely collapses — usually over-watered).

## CHAPTER 4: WATER ADDITION, ADMIXTURES & LEGAL LIABILITIES

This chapter will save you from lawsuits, sacked jobs, and test cube disputes.

### 4.1 The Water/Cement Ratio (The Chemistry of Failure)
- Concrete strength is governed strictly by the **Water-to-Cement (W/C) Ratio**.
- **Adding just 10 litres of water to 1 cubic metre of concrete can reduce compressive strength by 2 to 3 N/mm²!**
- If a plant designs a C35 mix for a structural suspended slab and you add 80 litres of water on site, that slab may only test at C25 in 28 days. The builder will be forced to rip up the slab at a cost of £50,000+, and they will blame the driver.

### 4.2 The "Water Added at Customer Request" Golden Rule
Groundworkers almost always want concrete wetter because it’s easier to rake. 
- **Rule 1:** NEVER add water without informing the customer that doing so voids the plant’s strength warranty.
- **Rule 2:** NEVER turn on the water hose on site until the site agent, foreman, or customer has signed the delivery ticket in the box marked:
  > **"WATER ADDED AT CUSTOMER'S RISK & SPECIFIC REQUEST"**
- Note the exact quantity added (e.g., *"Added 40 Litres at customer request. Signed: J. Smith"*).
- If the customer refuses to sign, **DO NOT ADD A SINGLE DROP OF WATER.** Discharge the concrete as batched or call dispatch.

### 4.3 Understanding Chemical Admixtures
Modern ready-mix plants use admixtures rather than water to adjust workability:
- **Plasticizers & Superplasticizers:** Increase slump from S2 to S4 without adding a drop of water, preserving 100% of design strength.
- **Retarders:** Delay initial set for long hauls (up to 3–4 hours) or hot summer pours.
- **Accelerators:** Speed up set times in sub-zero winter conditions to prevent frost damage.
- **Air-Entraining Agents:** Microscopic air bubbles that prevent freeze-thaw damage on outdoor pavements.

## CHAPTER 5: ROADCRAFT, LIQUID SURGE & ROLLOVER PREVENTION

Mixer trucks have one of the highest rollover rates in the UK haulage industry. Understanding why will keep your wheels firmly on the tarmac.

### 5.1 Centrifugal Inertia & Passenger-Side Rollover Physics
1. A loaded 8x4 mixer carries **up to 20 tonnes** of concrete.
2. **Drum Rotation Dynamics:** If looking at the drum from the front of the wagon, it turns **Clockwise**. But when driving the mixer and the drum is behind you in the cab, **it turns towards the passenger side (nearside / left)** for mixing and agitating.
3. **Weight Shift:** As the drum turns towards the passenger side, the internal spiral blades drag and carry up to 20 tonnes of wet concrete high up the **passenger-side wall** of the drum.
4. This shifts the truck’s dynamic centre of gravity **upwards and heavily towards the passenger (left) side**!
5. **The UK Roundabout Trap:** When circling a UK roundabout (turning right), centrifugal force pushes the vehicle's body and load outwards—to the **left (passenger side)**!
6. **The Compounding Danger:** The live concrete is ALREADY riding high on the passenger-side wall due to drum rotation, and turning right around the roundabout throws all centrifugal lateral weight onto that exact same passenger side!
7. **Result:** These two forces compound directly on the nearside suspension and tyres. Entering a roundabout or taking a sharp right-hand bend just 5–8 mph too fast will cause an immediate, catastrophic rollover onto the passenger side with zero warning or tyre screech.

### 5.2 Safe Road Driving Rules
- **Roundabout Speed:** Approach and negotiate roundabouts at **crawl speeds (10–15 mph maximum)**.
- **Braking Distances:** Live concrete surges forward under heavy braking. Leave twice the braking gap you would in a curtainsider or tipper wagon.
- **Camber Awareness:** Be extremely cautious on off-camber country lanes and road verges. If an outside wheel drops into a soft road verge, the rotating drum will roll the truck into the ditch instantly.

## CHAPTER 6: BATCH PLANT & JOBSITE MANOEUVRING

### 6.1 The Official Batch Plant Loading Protocol (Ian Henry - 30 Years Experience)

Loading under the batch tower is where concrete quality, driver safety, and legal compliance begin. Follow this 6-step protocol every single load:

1. **Precision Positioning Under the Batch Cone:**
   - Approach the batch tower slowly. Check your overhead convex mirrors and ground markers.
   - Check that you are placed correctly with the charging hopper sitting dead-centre beneath the rubber loading sock. Inaccurate alignment causes dry cement, aggregate, and slurry to spill over the rear hopper and mudguards, creating road debris hazards.
2. **Engage Drum to Mix & Set Revs (1,400 Engine RPM):**
   - Engage the drum into **Mixing Direction** (turning towards the passenger side / clockwise looking from front).
   - Set engine revs to **1,400 RPM (approx. 1.4k RPM)**. This drives the hydraulic pump to turn the drum at optimal charging speed (12–14 drum RPM), ensuring dry materials and water are drawn immediately down to the belly of the drum without plugging the throat.
3. **Fill the Water Tank (The Unbreakable Rule):**
   - While the wagon is being charged, attach the plant water hose to fill up the pressurised water tank.
   - **⚠️ THE GOLDEN RULE: NEVER LEAVE THE PLANT WITHOUT A FULL TANK OF WATER!**
   - A mixer driver on the road without water is a driver waiting to ruin a truck. You need water for site chute washouts, clearing sudden road splatters, and emergency activation of sugar retarders if caught in motorway gridlock or facing an engine stall.
4. **Primary Slump Check & Batcher Liaison:**
   - When the wagon is loaded, check the slump of the load immediately using the in-cab or rear hydraulic slump pressure gauge.
   - **If Slump Gauge is Inoperative:** Visually check the slump of the load rolling inside the drum.
   - **Liaise with the Batcher:** If the mix is stiff/dry, liaise with the batcher to add a controlled water trim. If the load is batched too wet, inform the batcher immediately before leaving the tower so mix proportions or aggregate moisture sensors can be recalibrated.
5. **Clear the Bay & Visual Ladder Inspection (The 3 Critical Checks):**
   - Once satisfied with initial slump under the cone, pull the truck forward out of the loading bay away from the cone to free up the plant for the next waiting wagon.
   - Set the parking brake, climb the gantry/chute access ladders, and perform a thorough top-down visual inspection into the drum:
     - 📦 **Amount (Volume):** Verify that the fill level inside the drum accurately reflects the delivery ticket volume (e.g. 8m³ full belly vs 4m³ half load).
     - 🎨 **Colour:** Check for complete cementitious uniformity. Ensure there are no grey dry aggregate streaks, unmixed sand pockets, or dry concrete clinging behind the spiral blades.
     - 💧 **Slump & Workability:** Confirm the true workability (S1 to S4) and fluid rolling wave before the wagon rolls onto the road.
6. **Drum Slow-Down & Proceed to Site:**
   - Once 100% satisfied with amount, colour, and slump:
     - For standard wet concrete: **Slow down the drum to transit agitation speed (2 to 4 RPM only)**.
     - For 0-slump / semi-dry concrete (ST1, Gen 0): **Stop the drum completely (0 RPM)** to prevent concrete balling and flash setting.
   - Secure ladder latches, stow wash hoses, and proceed safely to site.

### 6.2 Construction Site Reversing & Ground Conditions
- **Ground Assessment:** Housing sites in the North East are notorious for clay, mud, and soft fill.
  - Never drive onto uncompacted ground or close to open service trenches. An 8-wheeler with 32 tonnes gross weight will collapse a trench wall instantly.
  - Check manhole covers: Never drive over plastic inspection chambers or unrated manholes.
- **Reversing with a Banksman:** 
  - Ensure the banksman is visible in your mirrors at all times. **If you lose sight of the banksman, STOP IMMEDIATELY.**
  - Watch for overhead cables, scaffold poles, and building overhangs.

### 6.3 Chute Safety & Manual Handling
- Chute extensions are heavy and awkward. Always use two hands and lift with your knees, not your back.
- Keep fingers clear of hinge pinch points.
- Ensure chute hooks and safety travel latches are **100% engaged** before leaving site. A loose swinging chute on a dual carriageway is lethal.

## CHAPTER 7: WASHOUT RULES & ENVIRONMENTAL LAW

Concrete wash-water is highly alkaline (pH 12–13), equivalent to liquid bleach. It kills fish, burns vegetation, and causes severe skin burns.

### 7.1 Environment Agency Regulations
- Under the Environmental Protection Act, washing concrete slurry into road gullies, storm drains, ditches, or rivers carries **fines of up to £50,000 for companies and criminal records for drivers**.
- **Rule:** Never wash out onto the highway or into unauthorized drains.

### 7.2 Compliant Site Washout
- Use designated site washout pits, lined skips, or wash-water recycling sacks provided by the contractor.
- If no site washout pit exists, clean only the chute into your own chute wash bucket or onboard wash-water return chute.

### 7.3 End-of-Day Standard Drum Washout
- Add 500–800 litres of clean water into the drum.
- Reverse and forward the drum at high speed (14 RPM) for 3 minutes to clean the fins and belly.
- Discharge the wash-water into the plant's designated settling lagoons or reclaimer.

### 7.4 The "Stoning Out" Protocol: Sticky & Dry Mixes (Ian Henry)
If you have been hauling **dry, semi-dry, or sticky, high-cement mixes all day** (e.g. C40/C50, microsilica, polymer, dry-lean, or kerb mix), water alone will NOT remove the sticky cement mortar clinging to the blades and drum walls.
- **Why You Must Stone Out:**
  1. If left overnight, sticky paste hardens into permanent rock ("dag"). Within weeks, buildup can weigh **1 to 2 tonnes**, permanently stealing your legal payload capacity, straining hydraulic pumps, and requiring £1,000+ manual jackhammer de-dagging.
  2. Stoning out acts like an industrial scouring pad inside the spinning drum.
- **The Step-by-Step Stoning Out Procedure:**
  1. Request permission from the batcher or yard supervisor to batch a stone-out charge.
  2. Put **approx. 1,000 litres of clean water** into the drum.
  3. Load **2 to 3 tonnes of clean gravel / 20mm coarse aggregate** from the plant.
  4. Run the drum at high speed (12–14 RPM) in mixing direction for several minutes so the heavy stone scours the belly and blades.
  5. Reverse the drum so the gravel climbs up to the discharge throat, then switch back to mixing. **Run the gravel in and out of the drum a couple of times.** This ensures the aggregate scours both sides of the spiral fins and the throat cone.
  6. Discharge the dirty gravel and slurry into the plant aggregate recycling reclaimer, recovery pit, or aggregate bay.
  7. Finish with a quick 200-litre clean water rinse down the chutes.
